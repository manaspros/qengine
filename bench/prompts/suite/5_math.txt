A school is arranging 7 students in a row for a photo. Two of them, Ana and Ben, refuse to stand next to each other, and a third student, Chen, must stand at one of the two ends of the row. How many different arrangements are possible? Then answer two follow-up questions: (a) what is the probability that a uniformly random valid arrangement has Ana to the left of Ben, and (b) how does the count change if the row becomes a circle with Chen fixed at the top? Explain each step of your counting carefully and double-check the result with a second method.
